# Executive Summary

SQLI operates across three domains: Software Engineering, E-commerce, and Luxury.

SQLI's AI strategy is structured around two delivery patterns — Copilot and Autopilot — applied across two verticals: Software Engineering and CX & Luxury.

Copilot augments our professionals with AI tools. Our value remains skills, knowledge, and accountability. Volume will likely decrease; quality and profile seniority will increase.

Autopilot is the structural shift: AI agents execute the work, supervised by SQLI at critical control points. Our margin sits in the quality of that oversight — not in headcount. Pricing moves from time-and-materials to fixed price and unit-based models with SLAs.

The CX & Luxury vertical is our strategic wave to catch. As customer interactions shift from brand-controlled channels to agent-mediated interfaces, SQLI is positioned to design, build, and run the systems that govern those interactions — with deep business knowledge that hyperscalers cannot replicate.

The transition is underway. Orchestration tooling is maturing fast. The window to establish a differentiated position is now.

# Transition

## Orchestration Maturity

The transition to Autopilot Services is underway. Today orchestration is:

- Unstable
- Hard to debug
- Non-deterministic
- Tooling immature

→ Setup + maintain + evolve = heavy

Orchestration is currently moving from Emerging (one project – one orchestration) to Framework (best practices) and will soon move to Platform (standard).

The position at Emerging is critical to catch the wave, but it will soon become a commodity — like cloud.

What Will Remain Hard — Where the Value Will Sit

- Business rules
- Exception handling
- Data semantics
- Workflow design

"When do we escalate to human?", "What tone is acceptable?", "What personalization is allowed?", "What is a good answer?", "Is it brand compliant?"

If we stay on pure Software Engineering, we miss this part.

## Strategic Positioning

We anticipate the change in the structure of CX itself. We position ourselves as an advisory and future-proof partner: customer interaction with brands will be managed by agents. Agents sit in user-controlled AI interfaces. We will likely shift from brand-controlled channels to AI Assistant channels. The primary consumer of data is not the user — it is the agent.

Examples

- Shopping via assistant
- Support via assistant
- Content via assistant
- Decisions mediated by assistant

Two Systems Requiring Change

- CRM = system of engagement
- CDP = system of insight

The Transition

| Brand owns (From) | Entry points (site, app, email) · Interaction flow · Data capture |
|---|---|
| User owns (To) | Interface (AI assistant) · Aggregation of services · Decision layer |

What This Means for CRM & CDP

They lose:

- Direct interaction control
- Full visibility
- Deterministic journeys (user journey → agent workflow)

It shifts to:

- Identity & Access Control Layer — Permission-based data access: what data is exposed to external agents, how identity is verified, how context is shared.
- Negotiation Engine — Best personalized offers, personalization, pricing, context exposition (what is shared with agents).

Therefore, progressively, some website and app entry-point functionalities will be complemented by agent interaction contracts (MCP). SQLI will design these contracts: schemas, permissions, agent interaction workflows.

"SQLI will redesign your journeys to prepare for agent mediation." — Adapt CRM/CDP · Define data exposure strategy

"We assess and redesign your CX stack to operate in an AI-assisted / agent-mediated world."

Clients will ask: "Is this safe?" · "Is this on-brand?" · "Is this effective?"

We Build

- Agent Access Gateway
- APIs
- Negotiation logic
- Control plane — software layer that enforces, measures, and governs agent execution in production

We have the tech DNA to do that.
