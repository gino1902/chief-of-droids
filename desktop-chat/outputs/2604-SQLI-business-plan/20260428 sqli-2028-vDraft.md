Disclaimer — The objective in this document is to lay ideas as inputs for an action plan (costs) and a go-to-market plan (revenue). ## 4.1 Main Market Foreseen Disruptions

These foreseen disruptions are here to be challenged, adjusted, and used in return to challenge the rest of the business plan.

- Customer interactions with brands will more and more be managed by agents.
- Customers will choose products more; LLMs will copilot and sometimes autopilot the sales act (Yuka-like behaviour) based on consumer context.
- User digital endpoints will be complemented by AI assistants (specialised or not), which will become the consolidation point for product comparison and buy.
- Websites make business workflows navigable for humans. MCP servers make business workflows navigable for agents. It is a new navigation layer — coupled to existing web, APIs, data contracts, workflows, and security models — to design, build, and supervise.
- In the AI age, data security (not technology) will drive the use case possibilities, software and services manufacturing in our fields. (Points of control, human in the loop.)
- Trust model has to be revisited: how to trust an agent, what is done by agent vs human, what am I buying as an IT service provider client.
- AI will require new jobs specialised in agent supervision (security, exception management, hard gate management).
- Sovereignty and supply chain become primary concerns in the EU and must be taken into account in partnership ecosystem creation.
- LLMs will be standardised in cloud infrastructure and become a commodity.
- SaaS integration is decreasing fast in favour of workflow orchestration.
- Revenue distribution shifts from workforce & software chain value to Orchestration Value Chain.
- Coding languages will become secondary, in favour of algorithms and logic, and agent supervision. Managing a team of agents’ deliverables is as important as managing our own deliverables.
- A new workforce will rise for the next 10 to 20 years, with 2 different populations to address: AI-native new grads, and seasoned workers who need to get away from coding.

## 4.5 How We Are Helping Customers — Core Offerings

The intent is to define the core offerings in 2028 that represent the sweet spot between what an Agentic Journey Specialist should offer and the risks/opportunities our clients will face in light of the main AI disruptions.

### 4.5.1 A. CX Orchestration

- We run AI-enabled customer experience operations by orchestrating customer-critical workflows.

Our offering covers customer workflows, from acquisition and engagement to retention and loyalty, from marketing campaigns to product and service support — ensuring efficiency in content management, customer management, brand visibility, and customer experience consistency.

### 4.5.2 B. Agentic Enabling

- We support clients in shifting to the agent age by designing, configuring, and supervising agents.
- We create custom agents, or implement agent operating platforms (gong.io), data platforms, SaaS, or native orchestration layers (Anthropic, etc.).

Our offering includes agent-ready preparation of the data and agent navigation routes creation (MCP servers, adapters).

### 4.5.3 C. Agent-Ready Data

- We run data transformation to streamline our client data journey across their workflows.
- We define data exposure strategy, centralise the data under enterprise governance (trusted, secured, structured, agent-ready).
- We build data products — agent consumers — for better decision making, enterprise process copiloting, and autopiloting.
- We associate autonomous agents and data science to build predictive engines for customer experience.

### 4.5.4 D. Software Development Harnessing

- We deliver AI-enabled IT and software projects, secured, predictable, and human-controlled.
- We are offering a project delivery model based on AI orchestration (internal, client custom, or vendor) from project framing to UAT and RUN.
- We design custom solutions based on customer business context (data, metadata, and context layers), integrating the SaaS CX ecosystem.

### 4.5.5 E. Staff Augmentation

- We help clients in complementing their workforce by providing skilful profiles in software, cloud, and data engineering.
- We also provide talent skilled in workflow orchestration design, build, and supervision.
- We provide classic Staff Augmentation (mainly on site) on a T&M basis.
- We also provide flexible assignment on a unit-work basis (mainly remote). The client has a dedicated team to do its job, but the team can be shared by several clients to pay only for the time actually used. We call it next-gen Staff Augmentation.

### 4.5.6 F. Consulting

## 5.1 Glossary

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Agent consumers | AI agents or AI-enabled applications that consume governed data products to support decisions, copiloting, or autopiloting. |
| Agent mediation | Situation where an AI assistant or agent becomes an intermediary between the user/customer and the brand, product, service, or business workflow. |
| Agent navigation routes | Technical pathways allowing agents to interact with systems, data, workflows, APIs, and business rules. |
| Agent orchestrator | Coordination layer that assigns tasks to agents and manages tools, context, memory, permissions, handoffs, controls, and human validation points. |
| Agent-ready data | Data that is trusted, governed, secure, structured, documented, and exposed in a way that AI agents can reliably consume. |
| Agent supervision | Human controlled system-level monitoring of agent outputs, exceptions, risks, quality, security, and compliance before or after execution. |
| Agentic Journey audit | Assessment of customer or user journeys to identify where agents can mediate, automate, orchestrate, or supervise workflow steps. |
| Agentic Journey Specialist | Company positioned to redesign, build, orchestrate, and run customer/user journeys where AI agents mediate or execute parts of the workflow. |
| Agentic revolution | Shift from AI as passive assistant to AI systems able to plan, execute, coordinate, and monitor workflows with varying levels of autonomy. |
| Agentic workflow | Business workflow in which one or several AI agents perform tasks, make recommendations, trigger actions, escalate exceptions, and interact with systems under defined governance rules. |
| AI readiness audit | Assessment of a company’s ability to adopt AI, covering data, workflows, systems, governance, security, skills, and operating model. |
| Autopiloting | AI executes parts of a workflow with limited human intervention, within predefined rules, controls, and escalation mechanisms. |
| Copiloting | AI supports a human by preparing outputs, recommendations, analysis, or actions while the human remains responsible for decision and execution. |
| CX orchestration | Agentic operations of customer-experience workflows across acquisition, engagement, retention, loyalty, content, campaigns, service, and support. |
| Delivery orchestration | Structured management of delivery activities, tools, agents, playbooks, quality checks, and governance from project execution to run. |
| Hard gate | Mandatory validation point where an agent cannot proceed without meeting criteria or receiving human/system approval. |
| Human in the loop | Governance model where humans validate, approve, correct, or block agent decisions/actions at defined control points. |
| Predictive engine | Analytical or AI system using historical, operational, and market data to forecast outcomes such as win rate, staffing demand, revenue, or customer behavior. |
| Software Development Harnessing | AI-enabled software delivery model where project framing, build, testing, UAT, and run activities are accelerated, secured and controlled through AI orchestration. |
| Traceability | Ability to reconstruct what an agent, system, or human did, using which data, under which rule, with which validation, and with which outcome. |
