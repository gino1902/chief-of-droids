## Framing Question
<!-- full -->
By 2028, what capabilities must be in place for our delivery and operations model to become truly AI-native and differentiated?

## Workshop summary

### Delivery Centres
Network of Delivery Centres with 3 or 4 CoE as the tip of the spear (eg Data, Digital-to-shop, Managed services, QA).

### AI-native delivery model
Reusable assets, SQLI made or adapted, **charged to the client** as part of our added value model. Human owns decisions, using orchestrator tools to coordinate agent work.

### SQLI internal platform
Shared enterprise data and workflows as the backbone for the One SQLI way of working, with room for local adaptation.

### One way of working
Align on our internal operations ways of working, and also converge on new delivery practices (particularly PM and BA new roles).

### Knowledge sharing
Visibility on all POWs and Assets across SQLI, to streamline efforts and focus, and diffuse what works.

## Identified Priorities

###  Operations Platform
Deploy the modernised operations workflows and align ways of working, including external use cases like timesheets communications and client reporting.

### Industry knowledge
Complement delivery excellence with industry business knowledge, for a great understanding of our client context and data, to differentiate our agentic delivery outcomes.

### Delivery Orchestrators
Because there is no one-size-fits-all, create a place where the right agentic delivery toolchain can be tailored to each of our clients.

### All assets in one place
Gather all projects' proven assets in one platform, governed, spanning all offerings.

## Delivery Platform
<!-- divider -->
Three Pillars for a Delivery Platform to **increase SQLI services value**.

## Assets Collection

###
A SQLI Community governance is set up to monitor and surface the assets used for the activities SQLI delivers, from Project to Staff Augmentation services*, including sales offerings.

###
The community reviews and selects the best proven assets across SQLI projects (eg Bayard, Ceva).

###
The assets collection covers the end-to-end delivery lifecycle so end-to-end activities and deliverables are covered.

(*) Knowledge assets, deliverable assets, fitting to mission context and client industry. 

## Reusability process
- Selected assets are run through a quality assurance process, to ensure the best outcome with good predictability.

- Asset documentation is polished so it can be well understood and adapted by teams to real-life work.

### Adaptation to Context
At project start (or mission start for staff augmentation), the assets covering the whole SDLC and best fitting to client context are selected by the team.

The platform ensures the consistency of the selection and points out gaps and necessary adjustments.

A draft of the Project documentation can be generated, tailored to the assets selection and the client context.

For services relying on activity automation and repeatability (eg Managed Service, QA), **pre-built orchestrators** are available. This ensures consistency and quality across SQLI, enabling improvement at scale.

## And then what?
The first version of the AI delivery platform, the orchestrator setup, lays the foundations for a full delivery platform from tool to outcomes.

SQLI will build the remaining block to enable the monitoring of the value delivered (deliverables, time to market), the key performance metrics, including cost control.

This will bring the full feedback loop and enable the full SDLC improvement capability.
