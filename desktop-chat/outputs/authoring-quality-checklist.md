# Authoring Quality Checklist

Substance-side quality criteria for documents whose content is authored (ADRs,
BDRs, requirements, use cases, briefs). These govern the substance an authoring
skill produces, and are the counterpart to the expression criteria owned by
`editing-docs`. Adapted from the Clemson University Writing Lab revision
guidance (source below), taking the substance criteria that `editing-docs`
deliberately excludes.

## Authoring quality

- [ ] **Evidence sufficiency**: every claim is backed by relevant, directly
  applicable evidence, and nothing material rests on thin or tangential support.
- [ ] **Evidence integrity**: evidence is represented accurately, not
  cherry-picked, and contradictory evidence is acknowledged rather than ignored.
- [ ] **Source credibility and range**: sources are assessed for expertise,
  reputation, and objectivity, and the argument does not over-rely on a single
  source or a single type of evidence.
- [ ] **Analysis over assertion**: evidence is interpreted and its implications
  for the claim are made explicit, with the background and context the reader
  needs, not merely presented.
- [ ] **Counterargument and completeness**: likely objections and opposing
  evidence are anticipated and addressed, and the content fulfils the scope the
  brief or assignment requires.
