# SQLI strategic plan 2030, AI operations and delivery workshop, summary

| Date | Attendees | Framing question |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 2026-06-03 | to be completed | By 2028, what capabilities must be in place for our delivery and operations model to become truly AI-native and differentiated |

This workshop intended to explore attendees' ideas and the issues they encountered. The purpose was not to take decisions.

## At a glance

Where attendees converge:

- AI reshapes the delivery value chain role by role, and the human stays the gate, use AI but do not trust AI.
- Consumption is an architecture choice, prefer script automation, then a local LLM, then a cloud LLM.
- Deep client business knowledge is the hedge against AI erasing our lower-value activities, pointing SQLI toward consulting.
- One operations platform should carry shared enterprise data and workflows as the backbone, a minimal mandatory core with room for local adaptation.
- The loudest issue, scattered POCs with no visibility, reinvention, and no way to diffuse what works.

What remains to solve (different profiles in the room)

| Open fork | Where it stands |
| :--- | :--- |
| SQLI identity | Industrial delivery factory execution against consulting depth and value-oriented, unresolved |
| Experimentation | Having POCs bloom everywhere, uncoordinated (today's situation) against consolidate and diffuse, unresolved |
| Orchestration shape | One core orchestrator with client context against one per service nature (run, build, integration), unresolved |

## Delivery centres

Preparation converged that the low-cost capacity model is dead and value moves up the chain, with today's dev and design upskilled toward BA and quality.

In the room:

- Deep client business knowledge was framed as the differentiator that stops AI erasing our activities, with a push to build consulting capability, the luxury retail digital-to-shop team cited as a model.
- Moving team skills closer to the client's business, with tooling to learn a client's business fast and bring value fast.
- The staff-augmentation delivery model was questioned, alongside building SQLI skills from each client's context.
- Need to have a delivery centre network with 3 or 4 centres of excellence (eg Data, Managed services, QA, Mobile).

## AI-native delivery model

Preparation converged that the human stays the gate, new and upgraded roles emerge, orchestrator, AI coach, upgraded PM and BA, and that AI cost and KPIs are first-class. The earlier tension over what to hand AI now dissolved into a method.

In the room:

- Work the value chain role by role, and for each role split activities into human-held, AI-assisted and AI-delegated, delegating the simple tasks, the guiding principle being use AI but do not trust AI.
- Quality load shifts onto the BA role, more workforce is needed there, and today's dev and design are candidates to upskill.
- An agent taxonomy was drawn, SQLI agents for the delivery framework and assets against client agents that are contract deliverables.
- Token consumption is put under control by a 3-layer architecture, script automation first, then a local LLM, then a token-consuming cloud LLM.
- Enterprise KPIs were called for, team AI adoption and the share of projects delivered by AI.
- Open question, whether SQLI runs one core orchestrator with client-context customisation or one orchestrator per service: support, maintenance, project, integration.

## SQLI internal platform

Preparation offered five candidate platforms, and the room collapsed them into two: Operations platform and Delivery 'tools' platform.

In the room:

- Converge on one operations platform, driving training from employees' real project practice, analysing project leakage patterns, holding enterprise data so everyone sees the same thing, with workflows as the backbone.
- The platform sets a common minimal workflow and leaves room for local adaptation, no one size fits all, where minimal means the same trusted data, operating-model compliant and enterprise-policy compliant.
- Use of an internal tools platform to train people on skills, prompt engineering, delivery, roles, to reuse assets (the agentorium), to build orchestrators, to capture initiatives and POCs. Learning by using and doing.
- Data is not only internal, the operations platform must carry external use cases like timesheets and client committees, so internal, external and client data governance must be designed in.

## One way of working

Preparation converged on mandatory global standards for clean data, process mapping as the prerequisite, stronger control over AI output, and PM practice plus training.

In the room:

- Standardisation settles on a minimal mandatory core with local adaptation.
- As delivery gets faster and more iterative, quality is controlled differently and leans more on the BA role.

## Knowledge sharing and POC consolidation

This theme emerged strongly in the room.

In the room:

- Many POCs and initiatives are running across ways of working, testing, devops, release management, support and projects. (fact)
- There is poor visibility into what others are doing, a real risk of reinventing the wheel, and no clear read on how far the company has got. (issue)
- The few essential things each specialism absolutely needs were not identified.
- Open question, how to make people share what they do, consolidate it, give everyone visibility and diffuse good practice, without smothering local experimentation.

## Cross-cutting

The same four patterns recurred across every theme, role transformation, the AI-against-human gate, standards and process mapping, and cost with KPIs. They are the real workstreams, the theme labels are partly incidental. The human gate and the move toward deep client knowledge ran through the whole day. Value-based pricing was raised but parked, it carries into the next steps.

## Next steps (proposed)

These are study and analysis tracks, not assigned work.

| Track | What to study |
| :--- | :--- |
| SQLI identity | How to combine industrial delivery scale with consulting depth, and which client segments justify building consulting capability |
| POC consolidation | How to surface, consolidate and diffuse initiatives across the company without killing local experimentation |
| Orchestration model | Whether one core orchestrator with client-context customisation or one per service line fits SQLI delivery |
| Platform and data governance | The minimal mandatory workflow core, and the governance for internal, external and client data |
| Role transformation | A role-by-role activity breakdown into human, assisted and delegated, which profiles to upskill, and the adoption KPIs to measure it |
| Consumption architecture | Validate the script, local LLM, cloud LLM layering and model its cost by delivery context |
| Commercial model | Whether to move from day-based to value-based pricing, parked in the workshop |
