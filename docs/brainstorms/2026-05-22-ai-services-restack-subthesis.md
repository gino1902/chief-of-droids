# Sub-Thesis: Repricing the IT Services Stack Under AI Compression

**Audience:** Growth equity / PE
**Headline:** H3 — market mispricing on restacked revenue
**Status:** Working draft v0.3 — figures stress-tested; finance & investment glossary added

---

## Working canvas

The thesis emerged from a workshop on AI's impact on IT services delivery, then was stress-tested against the mechanics of how services projects actually run. The position carries two structural additions from framing — **asset amortization** and **risk re-pricing** — and the open points forward as the work plan.

### Position summary

Generative AI compresses engineering tasks 50–70%, which translates to **20–35% compression at the project level** once non-coding effort (requirements, stakeholder management, integration, UAT, change management, governance) is factored in. The first-order effect — m.d revenue erosion of roughly one-third over the AI transition window — is visible and being priced. The second-order effect is not: a new revenue stack (asset license, workflow-value, risk-graded delivery) is forming inside the m.d shell. Firms that build the stack while m.d is still flowing exit the compression with margin expansion, not margin collapse.

### Active lever map

| Lever | Status | Captures value how |
|:---|:---|:---|
| m.d delivery | Eroding | Activity billing on shrinking effort |
| Token saving | Tactical | COGS efficiency inside the m.d frame; realistic 1–8% of m.d depending on engagement intensity |
| Rate uplift via AI expertise | Soft lever | Premium on profile rates |
| **Asset amortization** | Strategic, untapped | Asset license / per-use, decoupled from m.d |
| Workflow-value pricing | Strategic, exception-coded today | Outcome billing on business value |
| Program / multi-project premium | Defensive, undervalued | Premium on integration & program continuity |
| **Risk-class pricing** | Structural, hidden | Reflects true cost; opens premium for HITL / audited delivery |

### Open points carried forward

| ID | Topic | Decision to make | Interacts with |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| A | Asset amortization model | Funding, license mechanism, IP P&L | Discount layer, exceptions, value capture |
| B | Risk re-pricing | Risk classes, methodology mapping, liability framing | D, delivery selector |
| D | Token pricing resolution | Billing mechanism with risk-class dimension folded in | A, B, m.d structure |
| E | Transition path | Dual-proposal evolved into client-maturity model with end-state | A, B, D |

C (unified pricing taxonomy) deferred — A, B, D, E will need a coordination layer to avoid drifting into incompatible mechanics.

## 1. The Core Premise

Generative AI compresses engineering effort 50–70% on coding tasks — roughly **20–35% at the project level** once non-coding effort is factored in. The market is pricing services firms on the m.d revenue that erodes as a result. It is not pricing the new revenue stack — asset license, outcome billing, and risk-graded delivery — that the same firms are building inside the m.d shell. The mispricing closes in **12–36 months** as restacked revenue lines mature into reported numbers.

This is not a compression-catastrophe thesis. It is a structural-restacking thesis: m.d erodes gradually, and the new revenue lines arrive on the same timeline. PE underwriting on the catastrophe narrative will misprice both the entry and the moat.

## 2. The Market Inefficiency & Macro Tailwinds

### Catalysts — why now

| Catalyst | What it does |
|:---|:---|
| Production-grade coding agents (2024–2026) | Shifted from autocomplete to whole-task execution; m.d compression becomes contractable, not experimental |
| Token-cost compression (~10× over 24 months at capability-equivalent inference; ~2–3× at the frontier) | Token-as-COGS now economic for full delivery; mid-tier models support 80% of agentic workload at fractional cost |
| Enterprise governance catch-up (2025–2026) | Security, procurement, and audit frameworks for AI-delivered work are now signable |
| Senior engineering scarcity | Mid-tier firms use AI agents to close the senior-capacity gap traditional hiring cannot |
| Premium-tier services-multiple compression (2023–2025) | Accenture-tier valuations corrected ~25% from peak; Capgemini modest; Atos extreme idiosyncratic; mid-tier mixed. Entry prices favorable at the right tier. |

### The mispricing — what the market is missing

- The market models revenue **compression** but not revenue **restacking**. m.d declines are visible; new revenue lines are not yet in reported numbers.
- AI-delivered revenue is treated as "m.d but cheaper" — when in reality it is a different revenue category with different unit economics (variable token COGS, asset amortization, risk premium).
- Incumbents are structurally slow:
    - Brand-rate ladders prevent visible m.d resets; firms discount silently, eroding margin without resetting revenue category
    - Partner-firm P&L (per-engagement) penalizes cross-engagement asset reuse
    - Risk underwriting infrastructure is configured for human-error distributions, not agent-error distributions
    - Sales orgs price m.d by reflex; rewiring takes 18–24 months
- Pure-play AI shops have asset depth but no enterprise sales motion, no risk methodology, no delivery culture. They cannot bridge to large-account revenue.

The dislocation sits in **mid-cap operators with delivery culture, latent asset-library potential, and willingness to break the m.d frame**. This is the entry profile.

## 3. The Unfair Advantage

The moat is operating, not technological. The model layer is commoditized. Defensibility lives in the operating system around the model.

| Moat component | Why it compounds | Why competitors struggle to copy |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Asset library compounding** | Each engagement contributes amortizable orchestration / workflow / domain asset; reuse rate grows non-linearly with library depth | Big firms suppress reuse via per-engagement P&L; pure-AI shops lack engagement volume to seed the library |
| **Risk-graded delivery methodology** | Proprietary mapping of risk class → HITL/MITL mode → indemnity reserve → token budget; improves with incident-history feedback | Requires operational scar tissue from real engagements; cannot be reverse-engineered from a deck |
| **Sales motion decoupled from m.d** | Bid teams trained to price assets, outcomes, and risk premiums separately; deal velocity holds as the m.d line shrinks | Big-firm sales orgs have 10+ years of m.d muscle memory; rewiring takes 2+ years |
| **Program-continuity premium** | Multi-project programs are where AI savings dampen and where integration / continuity premium is capturable | Big firms have programs but structure penalizes cross-program asset reuse; boutique AI shops don't have program-scale relationships |

The strongest moat is the compounding pair: **asset library × risk methodology**. Each engagement deepens both. A late-mover rebuilds both from zero against a moving target.

> ⚠️ Unverified — moat strength is asserted, not yet quantified. Asset reuse rate, risk-priced engagement count, and sales conversion data are required to harden this section.

## 4. Asymmetric Risk / Reward

Downside is bounded by the underlying services business (m.d revenue stream, defensible client base, normal services multiple). Upside is asymmetric: asset P&L emerges, multiple re-rates toward platform-adjacent comparables, revenue grows on non-m.d basis. **All ranges below are stress-tested replacements for earlier-draft figures.**

### Risk map

| Risk | Probability | Severity | Mitigation |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| AI commoditizes faster than asset value compounds | Medium | High | Build assets at orchestration / workflow / domain layer, not model layer. These survive model churn. |
| Hyperscalers ship vertical solutions and disintermediate | Medium | High | Focus on cross-platform integration, sovereignty-bound domains, custom data, and risk-graded delivery — categories hyperscalers will not enter |
| Big consultancies use scale to undercut once they convert | High | Medium | Speed advantage (already converted), domain-narrowness, willingness to break m.d pricing before they can |
| Client AI maturity grows slower than expected | Medium | Medium | Dual-proposal transition; maturity segmentation; parallel revenue lines so transition is gradual, not binary |
| Agent liability / regulatory shock | Low–Medium | High | Risk-class methodology (Open Point B); explicit liability framing in contracts; indemnity reserves |
| Talent retention as labor mix shifts | Medium | Medium | Redirect engineering capacity to asset R&D and supervisory roles; frame as mix reset, not reduction |
| Token-cost volatility | Low | Low–Medium | Risk-class pricing differentiates token budgets; pass-through / hybrid mechanisms (Open Point D) hedge volatility |

### Asymmetry framing (stress-tested)

| Scenario | What happens | Mix outcome | Multiple implication |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **Bear** | Asset library never compounds; m.d erosion proceeds without restacking | Asset + risk-priced revenue stays <5% | **0.4–0.7×** current services multiple |
| **Base** | Asset + risk-priced revenue reaches 10–20% of mix in 3 years | EBITDA expands via margin restacking; growth slows, quality improves | **1.5–2.5×** re-rating as restacking becomes visible |
| **Bull** | Asset + risk-priced revenue exceeds 30% of mix at 5 years; firm re-categorizes as hybrid platform–services | Recurring revenue character; differentiated growth | **2–3×** re-rating toward platform-adjacent comparables |

Asymmetry: approximately **1.5–2.5× upside vs 0.3–0.6× downside** on a 3–5 year horizon. Favorable but not absurd — defensible to an underwriting committee.

## Glossary — finance, investment, and services delivery shorthand

### Finance and investment

**Asymmetric risk / reward** — A bet where the magnitude of upside materially exceeds the magnitude of downside relative to the probabilities involved. Foundational to PE thesis construction.

**Bear / Base / Bull case** — Standard scenario framing for an investment thesis: downside (bear), expected (base), upside (bull). Each scenario should be internally consistent and probability-weighted.

**Business angel** — Individual investing personal capital in early-stage ventures, typically at smaller cheque sizes than institutional VC.

**Capital ask** — The amount of capital the company is raising, framed against intended use.

**Catalyst** — An identifiable event or shift expected to unlock value in an investment thesis (regulatory change, technology inflection, demographic shift, supply-chain dislocation).

**Cohort retention** — Measure of customer or revenue retention by acquisition vintage. Strong cohort retention signals recurring-revenue characteristics.

**Comparable / comp / Comparable transaction** — A previous transaction or publicly-traded peer used to triangulate valuation. "Multiples paid" refers to the EV/EBITDA or EV/Revenue ratios at which comparable transactions closed.

**COGS (Cost of Goods Sold)** — Direct cost of delivering revenue. For services firms, traditionally labor; for AI-augmented delivery, increasingly includes token cost as a variable COGS line.

**EBITDA** — Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, and Amortization. The headline operating profitability measure used in PE underwriting.

**EV / Enterprise Value** — Total value of the business including debt and excluding cash; the basis for transaction multiples (EV/EBITDA, EV/Revenue).

**Falsification criteria** — Observable signals defined in advance that would prove the thesis wrong. Required for a rigorous investment thesis; absent in soft narratives.

**Growth equity** — Late-stage private equity targeting profitable scale-up companies; typically minority stakes, lower leverage than buyout. Returns driven by growth + margin expansion + multiple re-rating.

**Hedge fund** — Pooled investment vehicle running active strategies (long-short, event-driven, macro). For public services firms, hedge funds may build long-winners / short-losers positions on sector dislocations.

**IP P&L** — A separate profit & loss view for intellectual property (assets, licenses) distinct from services revenue. Carries different margin and multiple characteristics from delivery revenue.

**J-curve** — Pattern where a company experiences short-term margin or revenue decline before delivering an inflection upward; common during business-model transitions.

**Mispricing** — Gap between an asset's current market price and the underwriter's estimate of intrinsic value. Investment theses are bets that mispricing closes.

**Moat** — Durable competitive advantage that protects margins as competitors enter. Operating moats (process, culture, methodology) contrast with technological moats (patents, network effects).

**Multiple (valuation multiple)** — Ratio used to value a company (e.g. EV/EBITDA, EV/Revenue, P/E). "Trading at 12× EBITDA" means enterprise value equals 12 times annual EBITDA.

**Multiple re-rating** — Shift in the valuation multiple a market or investor assigns to a company, typically because the perceived business category has changed (e.g. services to hybrid platform).

**PE (Private Equity)** — Privately-held investment funds taking majority or minority positions in private companies; thesis-driven, time-bounded, typically 3–7 year hold.

**Platform-adjacent comparables** — Public or private companies trading at multiples between pure services (low) and pure platform/SaaS (high). Used as reference points for hybrid business-model valuations.

**Recurring revenue** — Revenue contracted on a repeating basis (subscription, license, support) rather than per-engagement. Commands higher multiples than transactional revenue.

**Restacking** — Used here to denote the recomposition of a firm's revenue mix from one category (m.d-billed services) to another (asset license + outcome + risk-graded). Not a standard finance term — a thesis-specific frame.

**Sub-thesis** — A focused, narrower investment thesis nested under a broader macro view. Allows specific entry positions while remaining coherent with the larger market call.

**TAM-SAM-SOM** — Total Addressable Market, Serviceable Addressable Market, Serviceable Obtainable Market. Standard market sizing frame.

**Underwriting** — The discipline of evaluating an investment opportunity against return, risk, and exit assumptions. The "underwriting committee" is the institutional body approving a deal.

**Use of funds** — Specified allocation of raised capital across categories (R&D, sales transformation, M&A, working capital). PE diligence tests whether the use justifies the ask.

### Services delivery shorthand (for investor readers)

**Asset amortization** — Spreading the cost of a reusable asset (orchestration build, methodology, framework) across multiple engagements rather than billing the full build cost to a single client.

**Dual-proposal** — Sales tactic of issuing classic-delivery and AI-delivery proposals side-by-side, letting the client choose. Transitional mechanism, not a steady-state pricing model.

**Fixed-price (FP)** — Engagement priced for a defined scope at a flat fee. Delivery risk sits with the firm.

**HITL / MITL** — Human-in-the-Loop (human supervises every step) / Model-in-the-Loop (human supervises checkpoints, model executes between). Risk class typically dictates which mode is contractable.

**m.d (man-day, person-day)** — The unit of labor in services pricing. One m.d = one person working one day at a defined daily rate.

**Pass-through (cost)** — Cost incurred by the delivery firm but billed at cost (without margin) to the client; client bears the volatility.

**Risk class** — Categorization of an engagement by data sensitivity, regulatory exposure, and error impact. Drives delivery-methodology selection and indemnity reserve.

**Risk-graded delivery** — Methodology that adjusts mode (HITL/MITL), reserves, and pricing per risk class.

**Sales motion** — The repeatable process by which a firm sources, qualifies, and closes deals. AI-priced engagements require a different sales motion than m.d-priced ones.

**T&M (Time and Materials)** — Engagement priced by actual labor consumed at a defined day rate. Delivery risk sits with the client.

**Token / Token cost** — Unit of consumption for LLM API calls; the variable COGS of AI-delivered work.

**Workflow-value pricing** — Pricing based on the business value of the workflow being automated (cost of writing a contract, processing an invoice, handling a claim) rather than the effort to deliver it.
